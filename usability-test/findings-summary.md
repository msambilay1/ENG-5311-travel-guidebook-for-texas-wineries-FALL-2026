# Usability Test Report

## Test Report

Participants were asked to perform five linked tasks using the travel guidebook's current draft, creating a mock itinerary of their trip to a selected winery (or wineries). Participants were asked to speak aloud during their process of searching for information, while the observer wrote down additional non-verbal details observed. Participants were timed, but not rushed, to complete tasks, and were allowed to ask clarification questions of the observer during the task.

## Participant Descriptions

Participants were sourced by each member of the documentation project that were available to conduct testing. Ages ranged from a minimum of 21 to 45 years. Each participant used a digital format to access the PDF guidebook. Backgrounds varied in terms of whether the participants visited a winery before, or used a guidebook before. They also had varying priorities when it came to planning a trip.

## Findings Summary

The participants were in agreement that the information solely about the wineries and wine catalogues were helpful and allowed them to complete the tasks as instructed. However, once it came to the additional information needed to plan a trip to the winery, they found the guidebook lacking. 

Additional information that was requested related to the distance of the each winery from the listed hotel options; and participants also wanted recommendations for restaurants that might pair with the wine they'd purchased. While some winery descriptions mention a few dining options, it was not a substantial enough list to readily make a decision, and some participants had to use external resources to make a decision.

Overall, it seems that participants wanted a "one stop shop" for planning the trip, they did not want to check for outside information if it could be done for them.

When it came to accessibility, some participants noted difficulty using the guidebook in the PDF format. Through some devices, the Table of Contents did not work as intended and linked directly to the section when clicked, though the page numbering was correct. Most of the images, too, still did not have alt text or captions tied to them for the case of screen readers.
