# Page Template
Dimensions of travel guides seem to be traditionally expected at A5 (5.8 × 8.3 in)

Looking at example travel guides (fig. 1), the highly aesthetic experience of guidebooks is shaped at least partly by photographs. However, blank space can be used with a great deal of effectiveness. Also the use of lists, and breaking text entries into columns, will also make the guidebook adhere to audience expectations. 
 
Fig. 1. Guide template “Minimal” uses more white space, so there is slightly less emphasis on photographs. The bulleted list formats could be especially useful for our listing of wines from each location. Guide template “Travel” places more emphasis on the pictures, and the columnization of text has more of a travel book feel than the paragraph formats of “Minimal.”

# Screenshot/Image Rules


- Use a **primary** image for the venue. This should be involved in the header section of the winery’s first page.
- Optional to use **supporting images** to more fully establish the vibe of the location, within aesthetic coherence of the overall document.
- Use a picture of **each wine vintage** described in our wine section.
- Use **maps** for functionality. This includes labeling venue and most relevant street/road names, and using cohesive label imagery across the guidebook. (Maybe this is also optional/dependent on if the location is tricky to get to?)

# Accessibility Checklist

- [ ] Text-to-background contrast for readability. If the text overlaps an image, it doesn’t get lost in the colors or tones.
- [ ] Table of contents.
- [ ] Page numbers.
- [ ] Page headers & footers assist orientation.
- [ ] Scan for visual clutter.
- [ ] Clear, plain, direct language where possible.

# Design System Guide

- [ ] Keep related information visually grouped
- [ ] Maintain consistent spacing
- [ ] Consistent linking formats/colors for wineries 
- [ ] Use maps when they materially assist travelers and avoid cluster (show only pertinent information regarding general location and surrounding attractions)

# Notes, Tips, and Warnings

- [ ] Establish one consistent visual treatment for each category. Avoid using the warning formatting for ordinary extra information
- [ ] Notes
  - Supplemental information useful to the reader
- [ ] Tips
  - Optional advice that may improve the visitors experience
- [ ] Warnings
  - Information affecting safety, access, restrictions, or possibility of disruptions

# Typography and Heading
	
- **H1**- Winery/Vineyard name
  - Used once at the beginning of each winery entry
  - Largest heading
- **H2**- Major content sections
  - Used for major categories
- **H3**- Subsections
  - Used to organize information with H2 section
- **Body Text**
  - Follow established style guide
  - Consistent size, spacing, and alignment
  - Favor short concise paragraphs
